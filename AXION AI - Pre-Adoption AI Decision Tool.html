How it works

A gate-based decision framework.

Unlike a scoring tool, this is a decision tool. Each gate either clears, partially clears, or does not clear. Your final verdict - Proceed, Proceed with Conditions, or Do Not Proceed Yet - is determined by how many gates your organisation can honestly clear.

Step 2 of 7 - Gate 1 of 5

Strategic Rationale

1
Gate 1: Strategic Rationale
Can your organisation articulate a clear, documented business reason for adopting AI - one that would withstand scrutiny from your board, your auditors, and your regulators?
Why this gate matters: Organisations that adopt AI without a clear rationale are more likely to adopt the wrong system, at the wrong time, for the wrong reasons - and be unable to account for their decision when things go wrong.
Discussion prompts
→ Is there a written business case or strategic note approved by management?
→ Is this AI solving a specific, named problem - or responding to market pressure?
→ Have non-AI alternatives been evaluated and found insufficient?

Step 3 of 7 - Gate 2 of 5

Organisational Readiness

2
Gate 2: Organisational Readiness
Does your organisation have the minimum leadership, data, and people capability to adopt AI responsibly - or would deploying AI today create risks you cannot currently manage?
Why this gate matters: An organisation that cannot govern AI will not govern it well, regardless of how good the AI system is. Readiness is a governance question, not a technical one.
Discussion prompts
→ Is there a named senior leader responsible for this AI adoption decision?
→ Does the organisation have the legal right to use the data the AI system requires?
→ Have the staff who will use or be affected by this AI system been identified?
Assess this gate to continue
Step 4 of 7 - Gate 3 of 5

Risk & Ethics Review

3
Gate 3: Risk & Ethics Review
Has the organisation formally assessed the specific risks this AI system creates - to staff, customers, data, and reputation - and made a conscious decision to accept or mitigate them?
Why this gate matters: Every AI system carries risk. Accountability for those risks begins at the decision to adopt - not at the moment something goes wrong. An undocumented risk is an unmanaged risk.
Discussion prompts
→ Has a risk assessment been completed covering bias, data quality, and regulatory compliance?
→ Have the impacts on staff, customers, and vulnerable groups been considered?
→ Has the organisation reviewed the vendor's data handling and accountability obligations?
Assess this gate to continue
Step 5 of 7 - Gate 4 of 5

Adoption Conditions

4
Gate 4: Adoption Conditions
Are the minimum governance conditions in place - or committed to - before any AI system goes live in your organisation?
Why this gate matters: Governance built before AI goes live is always cheaper, faster, and more defensible than governance retrofitted after a problem. These conditions are the non-negotiable floor.
Discussion prompts
→ Is there a named accountable officer for this AI system beyond the IT department?
→ Is there a plan to train staff before the system goes live?
→ Is there a fallback process if the AI system fails or produces harmful outputs?
Assess this gate to continue
Step 6 of 7 - Gate 5 of 5

Governance Structure

5
Gate 5: Governance Structure
Does your organisation have - or is it committed to building - a board-level governance structure that will oversee AI on an ongoing basis after adoption?
Why this gate matters: AI governance is not a one-time exercise. Systems change, outputs drift, and regulatory requirements evolve. Without an ongoing governance structure, today's responsible adoption becomes tomorrow's unmanaged risk.
Discussion prompts
→ Has the board or governing authority been briefed on this AI adoption decision?
→ Is there a process to review AI system performance after go-live?
→ Will AI governance be included in the organisation's audit or risk reporting?
Assess this gate to continue
